## What is this? 

*Frankly, I'm not too sure.*

Much like the cover photo, I feel like Sam Flynn touching the dusty interface after it was left unused for many years. Having recently graduated from University and with -some- free time left, I started something I've been willing to make since I was in High School. A tech-related blog covering my love for computer science and its applications. 

Yes yes I know, how original, another tech bro website glorifying LLMs, AGI and AIcompany™ relying on milking the latest trends and em dash abuse. But that's not exactly my purpose. As I spent a lot of time trying out different things during my studies, I'd like to start documenting the learning process which, at times, is grueling but also rewarding. The cover photo I used is not random. In fact it is one of my very first inspirations for trying out programming: *TRON Legacy*. I won't go into depth yet, but the Tron series "infected" me with this idea of programming as an art form, a way to communicate with *the machine*. In a way, my interest is fueled by a mix of artistic influences and academic curiosity. My first interaction with ML (neural networks to be exact) was this [post](https://iamtrask.github.io/2015/07/12/basic-python-network/) by Andrew Trask, which at the time (March 2016 according to my Google history) went right over my head. But the curiosity and drive remained. 

This brings me to another important point. Publicly documented efforts by domain experts wrapped in a user-friendly, light hearted article form. Whether it is an Instructables DIY post, a YouTube video essay or a researcher's academic blog, I've always found invaluable information (more than what I can comprehend and utilize) in such places. Not Claude, not ChatGPT, but the efforts of other people that stumbled upon the same issues. 


So I'm starting a Technology Blog covering projects, documentation, learning resources, maybe series of posts regarding a specific theme, philosophical deep dives and so on. Part of it will be original, but another part will be just sharing other people's superior work (as I believe re-wording the same thing just to attract audience is producing slop without the AI). The main themes will probably include Control Systems, Machine Learning, Optimization and some Embedded work. I am certain it will be subpar in the beginning. 

> A disclaimer is in order: I am not implying that I make no use of AI-generated code for my projects. However, I dislike over-reliance and the current trend that labels any human actions in the software development loop as bottlenecks.

The fun thing is that this part of the world wide web currently has about `0` viewers. And it will most definitely continue as such, which works in my favor as the first few (closer to the order of tens) posts will probably be a mess. If I ever get to post this frequently.
